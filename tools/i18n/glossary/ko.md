# Loft Korean (ko) glossary

Address: polite 해요체 everywhere (apps, website, store), as Toss/Kakao/Naver write today: "~하세요", "~해요",
"~할 수 없어요". Short notices may end in a noun or ~함/~음 style only for status lines and labels ("최신 상태",
"로그인 안 됨"). Buttons and menu items: noun or verb stem, short ("취소", "계속", "다시 로그인").
Sentence case is irrelevant in Hangul; Latin product names keep their own case.
Typography: “…” for quotes in running text (‘…’ inside), one-character ellipsis "…" with no space after the verb
("수동으로 설정…"), half-width Western punctuation (. , : ? !) as Korean UI does, "·" kept where English uses it.
Particles after Latin names follow the Korean pronunciation: Loft는/를/가/로 (로프트), NAS는/를/가/와 (나스),
Mac은/을/에서 (맥), iPhone은/을 (아이폰), PC는/를, Windows는/를, Linux는/를, Python은, GitHub은/에서,
Incremental은/로. After a placeholder (%@, {name}) a noun is added ("“%@” 게이트웨이와") or a sentence is turned so
no particle follows the placeholder, never "이(가)" forms unless unavoidable.

## Tagline and recurring lines

- "Not the cloud. The loft." = "클라우드 말고, 다락방." Loft is the attic (다락방) in Norwegian; the Korean keeps
  the contrast cloud ↔ attic in two short beats, and "Loft" stays the name everywhere else.
- "Your files, kept at home." = "내 파일은 집에 그대로." (App Store subtitle without the period.)
- "Your NAS, on your iPhone" / "Your NAS, wherever you are" / "YOUR NAS, IN THE FILES APP" = "iPhone에서 만나는 내 NAS" / "어디서든 만나는 내 NAS" / "파일 앱에서 만나는 내 NAS".
- "The NAS stays in charge." = "결정은 계속 NAS가 해요."
- "Both treasury and best parlour." = "보물 창고이자 가장 좋은 응접실."
